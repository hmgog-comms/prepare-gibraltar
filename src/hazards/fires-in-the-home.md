title: "Fires in the Home"
summary: House fires can spread rapidly. Having working smoke detectors/alarms and a clear escape plan can save your life.
thumbnail: /assets/images/hazards/fires.webp
at_risk: Older adults, young children, people with disabilities or limited mobility, and those who are heavy sleepers or who use medical sedatives are most at risk. Properties without working smoke detectors/alarms, those with older electrical wiring, and people who smoke indoors face heightened risk.
tags: hazards
before: |

  - Fit smoke detectors/alarms on every level of your home and test them monthly. Replace batteries annually.
  - Make a home fire escape plan and practise it with everyone in your household, including children.
  - Identify two ways out of every room if possible, and agree on a meeting point outside.
  - Never leave cooking unattended — cooking is the leading cause of home fires in Gibraltar.
  - Do not overload electrical sockets or use damaged cables and plugs.
  - Keep flammable materials away from heaters, candles and open flames.
  - Never smoke in bed.
  - Ensure portable heaters are stable, away from furniture, and turned off when you leave the room or go to sleep.
  - Keep your address clearly visible from the street to help emergency services find you quickly.
during: |

  - Get out, stay out, and call 999. Do not go back inside for any reason.
  - If you need to pass through smoke, crawl low where the air is cleaner.
  - Before opening any door, feel it with the back of your hand — if it is hot, do not open it. Find another route.
  - Close doors behind you as you escape — this slows the spread of fire and smoke.
  - If you are trapped, close the door and seal gaps with clothing or bedding to keep smoke out. Signal from a window.
  - Never use a lift during a fire — use the stairs.
  - Call 999 as soon as you are safely outside.
after: |

  - Do not re-enter the building until the Gibraltar Fire and Rescue Service confirms it is safe.
  - Seek medical attention even if you do not feel injured — smoke inhalation can cause delayed symptoms.
  - Contact your home insurance provider as soon as possible.
  - If you have been displaced, contact the Housing Department for assistance.
  - Have your smoke detectors/alarms and any fire suppression systems inspected and replaced as needed before returning.
