title: Advice for Persons with Disabilities
description: Emergency preparedness advice for persons with disabilities, people
  with long-term health conditions, and their carers in Gibraltar.
intro: Emergencies can present additional challenges for persons with
  disabilities and people with long-term health conditions. Planning ahead —
  before an emergency happens — can make a significant difference to your
  safety.
sections:
  - heading: Make a Personal Emergency Plan
    body: >
      A personal emergency plan is a simple document that sets out what you
      would do — and who would help you — in an emergency. It should be tailored
      to your specific needs.


      - Think about how different emergencies (power cut, flooding, evacuation) would affect you and what support you would need.

      - Identify at least one trusted person — a neighbour, friend, family member or carer — who knows your plan and can assist you.

      - Share your plan with your GP, carer, and building manager if relevant.

      - Review your plan regularly and after any change in your health or circumstances.

      - Download our [Household Emergency Plan template](/downloads/) and adapt it to your needs.

        <p class="alert-box__heading">Personal Emergency Evacuation Plans (PEEPs)</p>
        <p>If you may need help to evacuate a building in an emergency, prepare a Personal Emergency Evacuation Plan (PEEP). PEEPs are also used in workplaces, schools and other public buildings — ask your employer or building manager about yours.   - heading: Prepare Your Grab Bag
    body: >
      In addition to the standard grab bag items, persons with disabilities
      should include:


      - A full list of your medications, dosages, and prescribing GP.

      - Spare prescription medications where possible — ask your GP about obtaining an emergency supply.

      - Any essential medical devices (hearing aids, glasses, insulin supplies, catheters, etc.).

      - Contact details for your GP, GHA specialist, and carer.

      - Copies of key documents: medical summary, care plan, power of attorney.

      - Any communication aids or devices you rely on.


      Consider including a completed <a href="https://disability.gov.gi/uploads/Documents/SNDO_Passport__8_.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Supported Needs and Disability Passport (PDF)<span class="visually-hidden"> (opens in new tab)</span></a> in your grab bag. Published by the Supported Needs &amp; Disability Office, it records in one place the information others may need to support you — invaluable to emergency responders and carers.
  - heading: Power Cuts and Medical Equipment
    body: >
      If you rely on electrically powered medical equipment — such as a
      ventilator, stairlift, electric wheelchair charger, or oxygen concentrator
      — a power cut can quickly become a medical emergency.


      - Contact **Gibelec** on [200 75957](tel:20075957) to register for their priority reconnection service. Registered customers are given priority when restoring power after an outage.

      - Speak to your GP or GHA specialist about backup options for your medical equipment during a power cut.

      - Keep devices charged and consider a battery backup or uninterruptible power supply (UPS) for critical equipment.

      - Know in advance who to call if your equipment fails — your equipment supplier, GHA, or 999 if it is a medical emergency.
  - heading: Evacuation
    body: >
      If you are asked to evacuate your home, having a plan in place beforehand
      is essential.


      - Identify in advance any assistance you may need to leave your home quickly — do not wait until an emergency to discuss this with neighbours or carers.

      - Complete a <a href="https://disability.gov.gi/uploads/Documents/PEEPS/Personal%20Emergency%20Evacuation%20Plans%20(PEEPs)_.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Personal Emergency Evacuation Plan (PEEP)<span class="visually-hidden"> (opens in new tab)</span></a> so that the help you need to evacuate is agreed and written down in advance.

      - If you live in a building with lifts, ask your building manager or residents' association about the evacuation procedure for people who cannot use stairs. Lifts must not be used in a fire.

      - Keep mobility aids, glasses, hearing aids and other essential items easily accessible — not packed away.

      - Know your nearest evacuation route and an alternative if the first is blocked.

      - If you are unable to evacuate without assistance, ensure your trusted contact and a neighbour are aware and have agreed to help.
  - heading: Advice for Carers
    body: >
      If you care for a person with a disability or someone with a long-term
      health condition:


      - Make sure you have an emergency plan that covers what the person in your care would do if you were unavailable during an emergency.

      - Identify a backup carer who could step in at short notice.

      - Keep a written record of the person's care needs, medications and contacts in a place others can find quickly.

      - Keep a completed <a href="https://disability.gov.gi/uploads/Documents/SNDO_Passport__8_.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Supported Needs and Disability Passport (PDF)<span class="visually-hidden"> (opens in new tab)</span></a> with that record — it sets out the person's needs in a format others can act on quickly.

      - Ensure the person in your care has their own grab bag prepared with their specific needs.

      - Speak to your GP or the GHA if you need advice on planning for specific care needs during an emergency.
  - heading: Register Your Needs
    body: >
      Several services in Gibraltar allow you to register specific needs so that
      support can be prioritised in an emergency:


      - **Supported Needs &amp; Disability Office (SNDO)** — can meet with you and help you make the necessary arrangements so that you are safe in the event of an emergency. Phone: [200 42196](tel:20042196), WhatsApp: <a href="https://wa.me/35056003195" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">56003195<span class="visually-hidden"> (opens in new tab)</span></a>, email: [sndo@gibraltar.gov.gi](mailto:sndo@gibraltar.gov.gi).

      - **Gibelec Priority Register** — for those dependent on electrically powered medical equipment: [200 75957](tel:20075957).

      - **AquaGib** — contact them if you have specific needs related to water supply interruptions: [200 41288](tel:20041288).

      - **GHA** — ensure your medical records are up to date and your GP is aware of your emergency needs. If you have complex needs that may require coordinated support during a major emergency, discuss this with your GP: [111](tel:111).


      For more guidance, download our <a href="/assets/downloads/vulnerable-persons-guide.pdf" download>Vulnerable Persons Guide (PDF)</a> — it also covers advice for elderly residents, children, carers and non-English speakers.
